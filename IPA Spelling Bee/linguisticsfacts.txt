The word “cliché” was originally an onomatopoeia. It came from the sound that old printing presses made while making copies.
Hebrew died as a spoken, native language around 200 CE. It was later revived in 1881, when a man named Eliezer Ben-Yehuda and his friends agreed to exclusively speak Hebrew in their conversations.
Pirahã is a Brazilian language that can be whistled, hummed, or encoded in music.
The English language consists of words, known as contronyms, that are their own opposites. For example, the word “cleave” has two meanings, i.e. “to cut apart” and “to bind together” which are opposites of each other.
The word "crisp" is pronounced from the back of the mouth to the front.
